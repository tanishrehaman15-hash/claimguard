## Challenge
Real-time AI on Transactional Data (Theme 1)

## What is the Problem you are trying to solve?
Insurers receive thousands of motor and health claims every day, and a meaningful share are fraudulent or inflated: padded garage and hospital bills, claims on policies bought days earlier, fake paperwork and organised rings that share phone numbers and bank accounts and stage accidents through the same colluding garage. Today most claims are checked manually, after they are filed and often after they are paid. Two things go wrong at once. Fraud still slips through, and the losses are passed on to every customer as higher premiums. Honest customers, who are the vast majority, wait days or weeks for money they urgently need after an accident or hospital stay. Rule-based red flags catch only obvious cases, generate many false alarms and cannot see hidden links between customers, providers and agents. Insurers need to judge every claim the moment it is filed, pay genuine ones instantly and stop suspicious ones before money leaves, with a clear reason for every decision.

## How are you planning to solve the problem? (Brief Overview)
ClaimGuard scores every claim in real time as it is filed. 1) A claim event (simulated motor and health feed, 60k claims) enters the scoring service. 2) A real-time feature store computes 26 features in microseconds: amount vs actuarial tariff, policy age, reporting delay, missing police report or pre-authorisation, plus an identity graph of customers linked by shared phones or bank accounts and rolling 30-day spikes per garage, hospital and agent, which expose fraud rings. 3) An XGBoost model, exported to ONNX, returns a risk score in about 0.2 ms. 4) A policy engine routes the claim: fast-track payout, one-document check, or hold for an investigator. 5) TreeSHAP gives exact reason codes, and IBM Granite turns them into an investigator note and a neutral customer message in local languages. A live dashboard shows the stream, flagged claims, fraud-ring graphs and model metrics. Stack: Python, XGBoost, ONNX Runtime, FastAPI, Streamlit, NetworkX.

## How does your solution leverage IBM Z / LinuxONE technologies?
ClaimGuard is deployed and running on a real IBM LinuxONE server (s390x, RHEL 9). The model is trained there (ROC-AUC 0.907, 6/6 tests pass) and the dashboard and scoring API run as always-on services, scoring each claim in about 0.3-1 ms. We compiled the full Python AI stack (numpy, pandas, scikit-learn, XGBoost, Apache Arrow) for s390x and fixed big-endian data issues so it runs natively. Production path: the claims system of record runs on IBM Z, so the model is served through Machine Learning for z/OS (Snap ML for tree models) on the Telum on-chip AI accelerator and called from the CICS/COBOL claims transaction before commit, so 100% of claims are scored with no network hop. Features live in Db2 for z/OS next to the claim records, so medical and financial data never leaves the platform. Pervasive encryption and Crypto Express HSM keys protect the data, and Granite runs in Secure Execution containers on LinuxONE.

## What value does your solution deliver, and who will benefit from it?
Honest policyholders get paid in hours instead of weeks: 37% of claims are fast-tracked with no manual work, while only 3% of fraud can enter that lane. Insurers stop fraud before payout. On a time-based hold-out of the 12,000 most recent claims, 99% of fraudulent claim value was held for checks, ROC-AUC 0.91. At the same alert volume, ClaimGuard catches 81% of fraud vs 62% for red-flag rules. Investigators get a short queue (4% of claims) with a 77% hit rate, explained reason codes, Granite-written notes and fraud-ring graphs, so they focus on real cases. Regulators and customers get transparent, explainable decisions. No claim is rejected by a machine, and customers never receive accusatory messages. Society benefits from lower premiums and more trust in insurance, which supports wider coverage. The same engine extends to crop, life and travel claims.
